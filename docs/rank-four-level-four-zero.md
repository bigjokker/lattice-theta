# Rank four: an even zero whose coset lattices are isospectral and not isometric

Curated copy (release 1.1.0, 2026-10-21) of the research report RANK4-LEVEL4-SEARCH.md of 2026-10-20. File paths are relocated to the release layout; the dated addenda of 2026-10-21 are included. Reviewed in [rank-four-level-four-review.md](../reviews/rank-four-level-four-review.md).

Prepared 2026-10-20 for item 2 of the workspace to-do list from a handoff
brief (neither is distributed). All numbers below are regenerated by the
commands in section 8; every claim is exact (integer and rational arithmetic
only) and is replayed by `code/verify_rank4_level4.py` with independent enumeration
and isometry backends.

**Question Q.** Does a positive definite rank-four lattice L0 have two
index-two overlattices O_v1, O_v2 that are isospectral but not isometric?
Equivalently, is there an integral rank-four lattice L with an identically
vanishing even characteristic whose two coset lattices are not isometric?

**Verdict: yes.** Take

    G = [[ 3, 1, 1, 0],
         [ 1, 9,-2, 4],
         [ 1,-2, 7,-2],
         [ 0, 4,-2,13]],      a = (1,0,0,1),   b = (1,1,1,1).

G is positive definite, odd, primitive, of determinant 1729 = 7*13*19. The
characteristic (a,b) is even (a.b = 2). Its sign kernel L0 = {x : x.b even} has
determinant 6916, and the two coset lattices L0 + Z(a/2) and L0 + Z((a+2t)/2)
are isometric to the Conway-Sloane lattices L^-(1,7,13,19) and L^+(1,7,13,19),
which are Schiemann's original isospectral pair of determinant 1729. Hence
Theta_G[a,b] is identically zero (two independent proofs at all levels: the
Conway-Sloane sign-change bijection, checked exactly on nine cosets, and the
project's weight-two modular cutoff B = 17920 with complete signed shells from
three enumeration backends), while O_v1 and O_v2 are not isometric (complete
basis-image search, replayed by integer boxes, plus an exact isometry
invariant). This is level 4 of the hierarchy in [rank-four-isospectrality-literature.md](rank-four-isospectrality-literature.md)
section 1: a vanishing with no geometric explanation inside L0 or outside it.
Aut(L) = {I,-I} and Aut(L0) = {I,-I}. The same construction gives eight
level-4 configurations, one for each known isospectral pair of Theorem-1 type
(section 5). No level-3 configuration was found anywhere.

Addendum (2026-10-21, from [project-assessment-2026-10-21.md](../reviews/project-assessment-2026-10-21.md)). "No geometric
explanation" above means no single Euclidean isometry: the Conway-Sloane
sign-change bijection is a piecewise norm-preserving bijection between the two
coset lattices, so the precise statement is that the vanishing is not explained
by one isometry between the cosets, whether inside Aut(L0) or outside it.

Correction to [rank-four-isospectrality-literature.md](rank-four-isospectrality-literature.md) sections 3 and 7: the note says the
Conway-Sloane configuration "has index three and does not qualify directly".
That describes their standard embedding. After re-embedding, L^- is isometric
to a 2-neighbour of L^+ (their intersection has index two in each), so the pair
does supply a half-integral characteristic zero. The note should be amended by
the reviewing session; it was not edited here.

## 1. Objects and the neighbour reformulation

Notation follows the brief. For L0 with Gram G0 and v in (L0/2L0) minus 0,
O_v = L0 + Z(v/2); the scaled copy 2O_v = 2L0 + Zv is an index-eight integral
sublattice of L0. Two classes v1 != v2 span a line {v1, v2, v3 = v1+v2}; with
L = O_v3 and the characteristic whose sign kernel is L0 and whose a is v1,

    Theta_L[a,b] = i^(a.b) (theta_(O_v1) - theta_(O_v2))

([foundations-and-exact-enumeration.md](foundations-and-exact-enumeration.md) section 8), so the zero is exactly isospectrality of O_v1, O_v2.
Levels: 1 some g in Aut(L0) interchanges them (a symmetry witness); 2 some g
maps O_v1 onto O_v2 but none interchanges; 3 isometric by no element of
Aut(L0); 4 isospectral and not isometric.

Two facts used throughout, both elementary:

- Level 4 is the same as a pair of isospectral, non-isometric lattices L1, L2
  that are 2-neighbours in Kneser's sense (L1 cap L2 of index two in each):
  then L0 = L1 cap L2 and L' = L1 + L2 form the Klein configuration. The
  third intermediate lattice L is integral whenever L1 and L2 are, because
  2 xi1 . xi2 is an integer; it may be odd even when L1, L2 are even.
- Given a known pair (L1, L2), "L2 is isometric to a 2-neighbour of L1" holds
  iff some index-two sublattice of L1 is isometric to an index-two sublattice
  of L2, iff some index-two overlattice of L1 is isometric to one of L2. The
  brief's two 15 x 15 tables therefore test the same condition twice; both
  were computed and they agree on every pair.

## 3. The certified configuration of determinant 1729

Construction (`klein_from_sublattice_match`): in the coordinates of L^-, the
isometry U between the two b = (1,1,1,1) kernels transports L^+ into the
rational span of L^-; L0 = kernel of L^-, O1 = U(L^+), O2 = L^-, L = third
intermediate lattice, L' = O1 + O2 (non-integral, determinant 1729/4). The
certificate then LLL-reduces L and transports (a,b).

- L: G above, LDL pivots 3, 26/3, 157/26, 1729/157; det 1729; odd (G11 = 3).
- Kernel basis K (columns 2e1, e2-e1, e3-e1, e4-e1), G0 = K^t G K =
  [[12,-4,-4,-6],[-4,10,-1,6],[-4,-1,8,0],[-6,6,0,16]], det 6916 = 4*1729.
- Complement t = e1 (t.b odd). Doubled coset lattices 2O_v1 = 2L0 + Za and
  2O_v2 = 2L0 + Z(a+2t), both of index 16 in L; their Gram matrices are
  divisible by four and O_v1, O_v2 are integral of determinant 1729:

      O_v1 reduced: [[4,-2,1,0],[-2,8,-1,3],[1,-1,10,-5],[0,3,-5,10]]  = L^-(1,7,13,19)
      O_v2 reduced: [[4,1,1,0],[1,8,3,-1],[1,3,10,-4],[0,-1,-4,8]]    = L^+(1,7,13,19)

  with explicit unimodular isometries (2 each) stored in the certificate.
- Non-isometry of L^+ and L^-: LDL sphere of L^- through norm 10 (largest
  diagonal of reduced L^+) contains 13 vectors, 2 of norm 4, 4 of norm 8, 6 of
  norm 10; the basis-image search finds no isometry. Replayed by
  `box_isometries`. Exact invariant: the multisets of theta prefixes (norm
  <= 40) of the fifteen index-two sublattices share exactly one element, so
  they differ.
- Identity, route (ii): `data/rank4-level4/cs-bijection.json`. With
  M = 2D4 + Z(1,1,1,1) and M' = 2D4 + Z(1,1,1,-1) (both of index 16 in Z^4),
  3M < L^+ < M and 3M' < L^- < M' with index nine on each side. The nine
  cosets of 3M in L^+ have representatives whose mod-3 patterns are the zero
  word and eight words of weight three; changing the sign of the coordinate
  divisible by three (coordinate 0 for the zero coset) maps each representative
  into L^-, the nine image cosets of 3M' are pairwise distinct, and each sign
  change is an isometry of every diagonal form. So theta(L^+) = theta(L^-) for
  diag(a,b,c,d)/12 and all admissible parameters, at every norm. This is the
  bijection Conway-Sloane sketch, now checked case by case.
- Identity, route (i): `modular_data` gives d0 = 6916, N0 = 3458,
  M = 16 N0 = 55328 = 2^5*7*13*19, index mu = 107520, cutoff B = mu/6 = 17920
  (norm4 units). Complete signed shells through B: 2,382,392 vectors in 2238
  shells, every signed coefficient zero. Three backends agree shell by shell:
  `exact_theta.complete_shells` (4,908,667 nodes, 12.6 s), the builder's
  integer Fincke-Pohst (2,436,511 nodes, 0.35 s), and the verifier's box
  enumeration with exact Cauchy-Schwarz radii (2,494,592 nodes). Verdict
  `proved_zero_by_modular_cutoff`.
- Aut(L) = {I,-I} (LDL spheres, replayed by boxes); both stabilize (a,b) with
  epsilon 0. Aut(L0) = {I,-I}: all fifteen classes are singleton orbits, and
  no element maps v1 to v2. Level 4.
- Adversarial tests (`tests/test_rank4_level4.py`, 12 tests, all pass): altered
  Gram or kernel, altered characteristic, removed group element or wrong sign,
  truncated or altered enumeration, interrupted replay (node limits 10 and 1),
  tampered isometries, a false non-isometry claim, tampered bijection pieces,
  tampered Task A cells and Klein data; each is rejected.

Relation to the determinant-23,716 example: that lattice sits at level 2 with
Aut(L) = {I,-I}; the new lattice also has Aut(L) = {I,-I} but its zero is at
level 4 and its determinant is smaller. The project's rank-four census bound
(det <= 24, all level 1) is unchanged.

**Correction, 2026-10-21 (determinant 280).** The determinant-4480 lattice of
the (2,8,14,20) configuration has every Gram entry even. Halving it gives the
odd primitive lattice G = [[3,1,1,0],[1,5,-1,2],[1,-1,4,-1],[0,2,-1,7]] of
determinant 280 with the same a = (1,0,0,1), b = (1,1,1,1). Scaling a Gram
matrix changes neither the vanishing nor the isometry type of the coset
lattices, so this is a level-4 zero; Aut(L) = {I,-I} with epsilon 0 on both
elements, so it is also a counterexample to the rank-four symmetry converse.
It is certified directly (zero by the modular cutoff, B = 3072, 383 complete
signed shells; group and non-isometry by complete searches;
`data/rank4-level4/det280-level4.json`, replayed by
`code/verify_rank4_det280.py`). The smallest known level-4 determinant is
therefore 280, not 1729, and the bracket for the minimum counterexample
determinant becomes 24 < det <= 280. No minimality claim is made.

## 7. Recommended next bounded step

Two directions, both with a clear completion criterion.

1. Minimality. The certified level-4 lattice has determinant 1729. Decide
   whether a smaller level-4 or level-3 configuration exists by extending Task
   D: generate all L0 with det(L0) <= D0 (a fresh LLL-reduced generator with
   the coverage argument of [rank-four-census-domain-and-cutoff.md](rank-four-census-domain-and-cutoff.md) section 1, since the
   existing one is certified only to 24), run `analyse_kernel`, and certify
   any candidate with the cutoff. The scan cost is dominated by isometry
   deduplication; measure at D0 = 48 and 96 before choosing. A level-4
   example needs an isospectral non-isometric pair of determinant det(L0)/4,
   so det(L0) >= 4*961 if the Conway-Sloane remark (x) suspicion holds; a
   level-3 example has no such floor and is the realistic target.
   Correction 2026-10-21: a level-4 zero of determinant 280 exists (the
   det-4480 configuration halved; see the note in section 4). Its coset
   lattices are not integral, so the 4*961 floor, which assumes integral coset
   lattices, does not apply to it.
2. Item 3 (smaller level-2 examples). For an even lattice M with a
   fixed-point-free automorphism of order three, M/2M is a two-dimensional
   F4-space and the five F4-lines are the orbits of the automorphism, so each
   line is a Klein configuration of three isometric overlattices permuted
   cyclically; it is level 2 exactly when no automorphism of M acts on the
   line as a transposition. The determinant-23,716 example is this
   construction with L0 = sqrt(2) M. A sweep over small Hermitian Gram matrices
   over the Eisenstein integers, checking Aut(M) and the five lines with
   `analyse_kernel`, would give the smallest level-2 determinant in that
   family. This is item-3 work and was not started.
